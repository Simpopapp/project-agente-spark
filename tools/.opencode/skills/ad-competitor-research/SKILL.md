# Public competitor ad evidence

Extends `ad-research` with the public landscape: public ad observations, landing-page evidence, and search-demand estimates it can turn into creative directions. It does not generate creative, modify campaigns, scrape private or gated data, or infer spend, targeting, conversion, or campaign type from visibility.

Narrows `browser-use` for two external JavaScript applications: when collecting fresh public-ad evidence, render the required Google Ads Transparency Center or Meta Ad Library lane before its agent-gateway search fallback, following the loaded `browser-use` instructions (Playwright or the `agent-browser` CLI).

## Saved research first

1. Before research or recall, use `marketing_memory_available` only if supplied for this request. Otherwise probe `knowledge://skill/marketing-memory/SKILL.md` with `code--view`. Earlier guides or availability values cannot establish current availability.
2. **Memory available, nothing saved:** Use the competitor-research guide, reading it with `code--view` when it is not already in context. A missing `/mnt/documents/marketing/` or competitor-research document means this pass creates it: complete the guide's save and read-back verification before the closing recap. The chat recap or a campaign brief's notes never stand in for the document.
3. **Memory available, document saved:** Use `marketing-memory` and its competitor-research guide, reading either missing entry with `code--view`. Read the saved document on each reuse request: users can edit Files outside the chat, so an earlier turn's copy may be stale. Reuse matching evidence without repeating research merely for a new campaign. In reuse replies, state the original capture date and inspected scope, and label new directions as hypotheses rather than proven competitor gaps. The guide owns which gaps need fresh research and how results are saved. Complete its save and read-back verification before the closing recap; say what was reused versus refreshed.
4. **Memory unavailable:** Keep the findings in the conversation and continue requested research without claiming they were saved. A recall-only request does not authorize a fresh external sweep; report unavailable recall instead.

## Scope

1. Establish the brand, product, conversion goal, target market, supported audience and need, one to five competitors, and each known competitor domain. When the flow carries a stored campaign brief, read it first; re-ask only missing essentials. Keep project facts separate from audience hypotheses.
2. When the user has not named competitors, build a small candidate set from the product category and public search instead of asking first. Discovered brands are candidates to verify, not confirmed ad rivals; present the list for the user to confirm or correct in the recap. When the user corrects the list, drop removed competitors' evidence and repeat the competitor-specific steps for changed candidates only.

## The Semrush lane

A fresh creative-research pass includes Semrush, even when competitors are named and the public libraries work: at most one `semrush--competitive_analysis` call and one `semrush--keyword_compare` call, no other Semrush calls. Reuse matching saved estimates with their database and capture date; a targeted refresh runs only the missing or affected calls. Call `tool_search` once for `semrush` when needed schemas are not already loaded. Skip the lane when the user asked solely to look up one named public library.

1. `semrush--competitive_analysis`, once: the brand's indexable apex domain (normalize a project-owned `www` custom domain to its apex), with up to three named competitor domains for organic keyword gaps, or no competitor domains to auto-discover candidates. Never pass a preview URL, shared-host project URL, unrelated parent domain, or product subdomain as the brand domain; with no usable brand domain, skip this call only, not `keyword_compare`. With more than three named competitors, pick the three most relevant to the product and market and name that sample in the limits. Returned brands and gaps are organic-search evidence only: verify candidates in the public libraries and landing pages, and never present a gap as a term a competitor bids on.
2. `semrush--keyword_compare`, once: five to ten distinct non-branded phrases in the target market's database, built from the product, supported audience need, conversion goal, landing-page language, and relevant organic gap candidates. Cover category or product language, problem or use-case language, and action intent when the project supports each. Exclude competitor brand terms, generic high-volume phrases whose visible intent does not fit the offer, and unsupported audience or product claims. Returned metrics are relative estimates within the compared set, never campaign settings or expected results. When the database is unsupported, or the brief names worldwide or multiple markets, use the single closest supported market and name the choice in the limits.
3. When Semrush is unavailable or a call returns no data, record the limit and continue without repeating the call.

## The browser pass

1. Before navigation starts, set the navigation call's `user_facing_description` to name the public ad libraries being checked and say it can take up to a minute.
2. Render both libraries for a fresh full pass, or only the requested or missing library for a targeted refresh, through `code--exec` with the active `browser-use` workflow. Google: `https://adstransparency.google.com/?region=anywhere&domain={domain}` when the domain is known, then the rendered search interface for the exact advertiser name when needed. Meta: `https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?active_status=active&ad_type=all&country={country}&q={competitor}&search_type=keyword_unordered`, with the market's two-letter ISO 3166-1 code or `ALL` when unknown or multi-country. Match results to the actual advertiser or page identity; ignore ads that merely mention the name.
3. One shared 60-second deadline covers all browser work, started before the first library navigation; every advertiser, retry, scroll, screenshot, and revisit counts against it. Set a 20-second timeout on every navigation or interaction, shortened to the remaining budget when smaller. Retry once only on a transient loading failure, and spend at most 30 seconds on one library including retries. Playwright: run the capture helper from step 4 once per competitor and library, with `--deadline` set to the seconds left on the shared budget or on that library's 30 seconds, whichever is smaller, and visit any page it does not cover (a landing page, an advertiser search) in one further script that saves the rendered text and screenshots and hard-stops with `asyncio.wait_for(..., timeout=<seconds left>)`; never a wrapper with a larger budget, no per-page timeouts summed into a larger outer timeout, and no further script that navigates a library page the helper already captured. `agent-browser`: check the same shared deadline before every call instead of resetting a budget per command. After the pass returns, later `code--exec` calls in the same turn may inspect saved captures but never reopen either library; a later turn that must refresh the evidence starts a new pass under a fresh 60-second deadline.
4. Capture each library page with the bundled helper instead of hand-written screenshot logic: `code--exec` `cp /tmp/knowledge/skill/ad-competitor-research/scripts/capture_ads.py /tmp/capture_ads.py` once, then once per competitor and library `python3 /tmp/capture_ads.py --library google|meta --url "<the URL from step 2>" --out /tmp/browser/<library>-<competitor> --match "<advertiser or page name>" --deadline <seconds left, at most 30>`. For up to four matching ad cards it writes a JPEG screenshot of the card alone, saves the original creative when the page exposes one (Google image ads, Meta image ads, and the poster frame of a Meta video), keeps every file under 500 KB, and records advertiser or page identity, Library or creative id, copy, format, per-ad source URL, the Meta start date, and `match_basis` (`advertiser`, or `copy` when the card showed no readable identity) in `<out>/<library>-ads.json`; its last line says `captured N ad(s)`, `blocked`, `no cards matched`, `no ad cards rendered`, or `stopped early` / `stopped at the deadline`, prefixed with `captured N ad(s);` when some cards were saved first. A `copy` match is a mention until the card screenshot confirms the advertiser. Open each capture you will cite with `code--view`; CTA, destination, Google dates, and platforms come from the card screenshot, not the manifest. Prefer the original creative as the figure and the card screenshot when there is none; a poster frame is one frame of a video, and the caption says so. **Helper unavailable or failing:** when the copy fails, the script exits without a manifest, or the page structure changed so no card is isolated, take one JPEG element or clipped screenshot per creative yourself (`type="jpeg"`, `quality=70`, under 500 KB) under `/tmp/browser/`, and never embed a crop you have not confirmed shows one ad. In the `agent-browser` lane, whose `screenshot` writes a full-viewport PNG, embed a capture only when it is under that cap and otherwise record the missing figure under open gaps. Both libraries and most landing pages render client-side, so an empty or near-empty text extraction is not evidence of no ads or no page: read the capture, and mark the lane unavailable when the capture shows nothing usable. Never send the library URLs through agent-gateway website fetch.
5. Stop a library immediately on a login wall, CAPTCHA, access block, or rate limit, with no retry, and record it as unavailable. When the bounded attempts instead return no usable matching ads, record that. In either case fall back to agent-gateway web search (`"{competitor} ads"`, `"{competitor} Meta Ad Library"`, `site:adstransparency.google.com {competitor}`) and the Web Archive for positioning changes over time. Name access failures in the limits and label fallback snippets by their real source, never as library observations. Report a library whose browser pass did not run as not checked — blocked and unavailable are claims about a page that was opened.
6. Inspect each researched competitor's current landing page even when the libraries return usable ads: hero headline, CTA, form asks, social proof, and continuity from any observed ad destination, saving the hero region as a JPEG capture under the same rule. For strategy context, capture the public value proposition, differentiators, and any inspected product/pricing or content-channel evidence. Interpret these as positioning signals, not knowledge of internal strategy. Reuse dated matching page evidence when this is a targeted refresh of another lane.

## Evidence discipline

1. Cluster only directly observed patterns: message structure, product representation, composition, format, CTA, destination, ad-to-page continuity. Keep project facts, public-ad observations, landing-page observations, Semrush estimates, and hypotheses distinct.
2. A saved screenshot is visual evidence only after opening it with `code--view`; omit visual claims from unopened captures. Reconcile captured text and screenshots before the recap. When a later or more specific result conflicts with an earlier one, report the conflict or use the more specific evidence.
3. Describe a partial result as an inspected sample and list what was visible. Describe only the creative previews actually inspected, never the advertiser's whole creative set.
4. Label every audience, competitive, or campaign implication as your interpretation and name the project or external evidence behind it. Never attribute a derived claim to a source that did not provide it, and never use one evidence lane alone to claim competitor behavior or campaign performance.
5. A claim about a named brand requires a source captured in this conversation or a saved observation read from marketing memory with its source URL and capture date; quote exact wording or label a paraphrase. An unsupported brand claim goes under hypotheses.
6. A capture is evidence for the date it was taken. Before presenting an earlier turn's or saved capture as current, run the affected lane again as this turn's pass or label the claim with its original capture date. Saving or reading a document does not refresh its sources.

## The handoff

Structure the findings as five sections for `ad-research`, separate from the chat recap. When marketing memory is available, persist reusable evidence through its competitor-research guide rather than leaving it only in conversation:

- **Public ad observations:** cited facts with source links.
- **Landing-page observations:** current message, proof, CTA, and ad-to-page continuity.
- **Search-demand estimates:** the most decision-relevant Semrush comparisons, named as estimates with the target market.
- **Unknowns and limits:** unavailable libraries, unverified placements, missing evidence.
- **Patterns, whitespace hypotheses, and two to three original directions:** built from the user's brand proof and structural insights; never a competitor's wording, identity, product imagery, or logo.

The chat message:

1. At most five plain-language bullets, one or two short sentences each, in this order: who was checked and what their public ads show; what their landing pages show; what people search for; the strongest opening for this brand; what could not be verified.
2. Name Semrush and the target market next to every search figure in chat, and keep the search bullet to plain comparisons of the returned numbers. Keep raw CPC, competition, and difficulty in the working material unless the user asks; on a keyword-detail ask, show the exact returned Semrush fields in a compact table, with agent-created groupings visibly separate and labelled as interpretation.
3. Label the strongest-opening bullet as your interpretation and name the project or external evidence behind it. Every implication drawn from the evidence (audience fit, entry point, gap, whitespace) goes in that bullet only, never stated as fact in an observation or estimate bullet or a closing line.
4. Keep source links inline. No marketing jargon, fetch or scraping mechanics, or option menus in chat.
5. The bullets are the reply's closing content, followed in the campaign create flow only by the ads conduct knowledge's Continue button (its message naming the next step), and the research turn ends there; text followed by another tool call in the same reply never reaches the user. Open no question card in the research turn: `ad-research` validates the sections and carries the evidence into the creative kickoff next turn.

A generated document or report:

1. Carries the evidence discipline of the working sections: every ad-presence claim traces to a capture, and unchecked or unavailable lanes are named inside the document.
2. Lists in its sources line only the lanes that produced a capture for it, each with the capture date. A lane that was not run for the document goes under its limits instead.
